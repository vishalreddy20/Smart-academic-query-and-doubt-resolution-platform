Complexity of Quantum Entanglement in Macroscopic Systems

Elara Vance

Submitted Oct 24, 2024 • Academic Tier: Graduate Research

I am exploring the theoretical limits of decoherence when entanglement is scaled to macroscopic objects. Specifically, how do we reconcile the Penrose interpretation of gravity-induced collapse with the experimental results seen in recent optomechanical resonator tests?

The current literature seems to diverge on whether the "objective reduction" happens at a specific mass threshold or if it is a continuous degradation of the wave function relative to environmental noise. Any clarification on the mathematical modeling for this transition would be invaluable.

Resolved on Oct 26, 2024

Dr. Aris Thorne

Professor of Theoretical Physics • Senior Curator

To address the core of your query, Elara, the divergence you’ve observed stems from the fundamental tension between Standard Quantum Mechanics and the Objective Reduction (OR) models proposed by Penrose and Hameroff.

The mathematical resolution lies in the Diósi-Penrose criterion, where the collapse time \(\tau_G\) is inversely proportional to the gravitational self-energy difference of the displaced mass distribution. In macroscopic systems, this energy difference becomes significant enough to force a collapse in timescales shorter than environmental decoherence.

science Deep Dive: Gravitational Coupling

The recent experiments with optomechanical resonators (specifically the 2023 Leiden study) suggest that while the environment is the primary driver of decoherence, there is a "noise floor" that cannot be explained without incorporating gravitational self-interaction. We model this as a non-linear addition to the Schrödinger equation, effectively bounding the superposition lifetime.
